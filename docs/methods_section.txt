BULK RNA-SEQ ANALYSIS

Sequencing and read processing
<Cell lines, constructs, RNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing to
be added.> RNA from four conditions (TP53/ATRX double knockout clone E6; E6
transduced with an empty vector, with ATRX full length, or with an ATRX in-frame
fusion construct; three replicates each) was sequenced as 150-bp paired-end,
reverse-stranded reads. Read quality was assessed with FastQC (v0.12.1; Andrews,
2010) and MultiQC (v1.25.2; Ewels et al., 2016) before and after trimming.
Adapters and low-quality bases (Phred < 20) were removed with Trim Galore
(v0.6.10; Krueger, 2023) and Cutadapt (v4.9; Martin, 2011), and reads shorter
than 20 nt were discarded. Reads were aligned with STAR (v2.7.11b; Dobin et al.,
2013) in two-pass mode to the human genome (hg38, <annotation source and
release>) supplemented with the sequences of the two ATRX constructs and of the
EGFP and mCherry reporters. Read pairs overlapping exons were counted per gene
with featureCounts (Subread v2.0.6; Liao et al., 2014), retaining uniquely
mapped reads with mapping quality ≥ 10. The workflow was implemented in
Snakemake (v8; Mölder et al., 2021), and all downstream analyses were performed
in R (v4.4.2; R Core Team, 2024), with figures generated using ggplot2 (v3.5.1;
Wickham, 2016).

Quantification of ATRX expression
Reads from the ATRX constructs map equally well to the endogenous ATRX locus and
are therefore discarded as multi-mapping reads by unique counting. Total ATRX
expression was quantified by pooling the exons of endogenous ATRX and of both
constructs into a single feature and re-counting the alignments with
multi-mapping reads fractionally assigned (featureCounts -M --fraction).
Expression of the two constructs was compared using reads overlapping (≥ 25 nt)
the Twin-Strep/10×His tag sequence, which is shared by both constructs and
absent from the human genome. For all downstream analyses, the pooled ATRX count
(rounded to integers) replaced the endogenous and construct features, and the
reporter features were removed. ATRX was excluded from all reported differential
expression results.

Differential expression analysis
Differential expression analysis was performed with DESeq2 (v1.46.0; Love et
al., 2014) using a single model (~ condition) fitted to the 12 E6-derived
samples, after removing genes with fewer than 10 reads across samples (<N> of
<N> genes retained). Contrasts compared the empty vector control with E6, each
ATRX construct with the empty vector control, and the two ATRX constructs with
each other. Significance was assessed with Wald tests, P values were adjusted
using the Benjamini–Hochberg method (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995), and genes with
an adjusted P value (FDR) < 0.05 and |log2 fold-change| ≥ 1 were considered
differentially expressed.

Principal component analysis (PCA)
Counts were variance-stabilised with DESeq2 (blind to the experimental design),
and PCA was performed on the 500 most variable genes (centred but not scaled)
after removal of ATRX, for the 12 E6-derived samples. Sample similarity was
assessed by Spearman correlation of variance-stabilised counts, excluding ATRX,
construct and reporter features.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)
Pre-ranked GSEA (Subramanian et al., 2005) was performed with fgsea (v1.32.0,
fgseaMultilevel; Korotkevich et al., 2021) on genes ranked by the DESeq2 Wald
statistic. Hallmark (Liberzon et al., 2015) and Gene Ontology Biological Process
(GO-BP; Ashburner et al., 2000) gene sets were obtained from MSigDB (v7.5.1;
Liberzon et al., 2011) using msigdbr (v7.5.1; Dolgalev, 2022). Current human
KEGG pathways (Kanehisa & Goto, 2000) were retrieved using KEGGREST (<version>;
Tenenbaum, 2017; retrieved <date>) and mapped to gene symbols with org.Hs.eg.db
(v3.20.0; Carlson, 2017). Two custom gene sets (all HOX genes; HOXB genes) were
added. Gene sets with 9–500 detected genes were tested and considered
significantly enriched at FDR < 0.05. For the comparison between the two ATRX
constructs, KEGG and GO-BP results were further grouped into five functional
categories (replication and repair, chromosome, cell growth and death, nervous
system, development and regeneration), defined by the KEGG pathway
classification and, for GO-BP, by keyword matching of term names.

Genes and pathways shared by both ATRX constructs
Genes differentially expressed in the same direction in both construct vs empty
vector comparisons were tested for over-representation in Hallmark, KEGG and
GO-BP gene sets (gene set size range: 10–500) using one-sided hypergeometric
tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, with all genes tested in both
comparisons as the background universe. Given the small number of shared genes,
gene sets with nominal P < 0.05 were reported as exploratory. Pathways were
considered shared when significantly enriched (FDR < 0.05) with the same sign of
the normalised enrichment score in the GSEA of both comparisons.

Integration with co-IP proteomics
Transcriptomic differences between the two ATRX constructs were compared with
proteins differentially enriched between the constructs in the co-IP/MS analysis
(S1744 vs S1011; FDR ≤ 0.05 and |log2 fold-change| > 1), matched by gene symbol.
RNA and protein changes were considered concordant when their log2 fold-changes
had the same sign.

Neuroblastoma patient cohort
Bulk RNA-seq data from an in-house neuroblastoma cohort (<reference; ethics
approval>) had previously been trimmed with fastp (Chen et al., 2018),
quantified with Salmon (Patro et al., 2017), summarised to genes with tximeta
(Love et al., 2020) and filtered with edgeR filterByExpr (Robinson et al., 2010;
Chen et al., 2016), retaining 14,604 of 38,269 genes. Normalised counts were
used without correction for statistical testing, and after removal of the
library layout effect (single-end unstranded, single-end stranded, paired-end)
with limma removeBatchEffect (Ritchie et al., 2015), with sample material
retained in the design, for visualisation only. Values were log2(x +
1)-transformed after setting negative values to zero. Tumour samples were
classified as ATRX-deleted, MYCN-amplified (ATRX wild type) or ATRX and MYCN
wild type, and assigned to primary tumour or bone marrow disseminated tumour
cells (DTC) at diagnosis, treatment or relapse; tumour-free bone marrow served
as control. Samples lacking a classification, tissue, time point or library
layout annotation, or absent from either expression matrix, were excluded (<N>
samples from <N> patients included). Analyses were performed on five overlapping
sample sets: all tumour samples, primary tumours at diagnosis, all DTC samples,
DTC at diagnosis and DTC at relapse. PCA was performed on the 2,000 most
variable genes among those with log2 expression > 1 in at least two samples.

Expression of candidate ATRX interactors
Candidate direct interactors of each ATRX construct were selected from
co-IP/MS-enriched proteins by AlphaFold2 interaction modelling (Jumper et al.,
2021; <reference to methods>). Expression of the corresponding genes was
examined as log2(normalised counts + 1) in the E6-derived samples and as
layout-corrected log2 expression in the patient cohort, for each tumour group
and sample set, with control bone marrow shown for reference. Samples from the
same patient within a sample set were averaged.

Differential expression and pathway analysis in patients
Differential expression between the three tumour groups was analysed separately
in each sample set with limma (v3.62.1; Ritchie et al., 2015) on uncorrected
log2(normalised counts + 1), using limma-trend (Law et al., 2014) with robust
empirical Bayes moderation (Phipson et al., 2016). The design included tumour
group and library layout and, in the pooled sample sets, sample category
(sum-to-zero coding). Where patients contributed several samples, patient was
treated as a blocking factor, with the within-patient correlation estimated
using duplicateCorrelation (Smyth et al., 2005). Genes with log2 expression > 1
in at least as many samples as the smallest group were tested. Pairwise
comparisons between groups with at least two samples each were extracted as
contrasts, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and genes
with FDR < 0.05 and |log2 fold-change| ≥ 1 were considered differentially
expressed. Control bone marrow was not tested.
For each comparison, pre-ranked GSEA was performed with fgsea on genes ranked by
the moderated t-statistic, using the Hallmark, GO-BP and KEGG gene sets and size
limits described above. In addition, pathway activity was scored per sample with
GSVA (v2.0.0, Gaussian kernel; Hänzelmann et al., 2013) and the scores were
fitted with the same limma design. Differences across the three tumour groups
were tested with a moderated F-test (Smyth, 2004), followed by pairwise
contrasts with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within each pair.
