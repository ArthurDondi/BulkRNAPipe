Bulk RNA-seq Analysis

Sequencing and read processing

<Cell lines, constructs, RNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing to
be added.>. Read quality was assessed with FastQC (v0.12.1; Andrews, 2010) and
MultiQC (v1.25.2; Ewels et al., 2016) before and after trimming. Adapter
sequences and low-quality 3′ ends (Phred < 20) were trimmed with Trim Galore
(v0.6.10; Krueger et al., 2023), a wrapper around Cutadapt (v4.9; Martin, 2011),
and read pairs in which either read was shorter than 20 nt were discarded. Reads
were aligned with STAR (v2.7.11b; Dobin et al., 2013) in two-pass mode to the
human reference genome hg38 from the UCSC Genome Browser, with the UCSC RefSeq
gene annotation (10 January 2020), supplemented with the sequences of the two
ATRX constructs and of the EGFP and mCherry reporters. Read pairs overlapping
exons were counted per gene with featureCounts (Subread v2.0.6; Liao et al.,
2014), retaining uniquely mapped reads with mapping quality ≥ 10. The workflow
was implemented in Snakemake (v8; Mölder et al., 2021), and all downstream
analyses were performed in R (v4.4.2), with figures generated using ggplot2
(v3.5.1).

Quantification of ATRX expression

Reads from the ATRX constructs map equally well to the endogenous ATRX locus and
are therefore discarded as multi-mapping reads by unique counting. Total ATRX
expression was quantified by pooling the exons of endogenous ATRX and of both
constructs into a single feature and re-counting the alignments with
multi-mapping reads fractionally assigned (featureCounts -M --fraction).
Expression of the two constructs was compared using reads overlapping (≥ 25 nt)
the Twin-Strep/10×His tag sequence, which is shared by both constructs and
absent from the human genome. For all downstream analyses, the pooled ATRX count
(rounded to integers) replaced the endogenous and construct features, and the
reporter features were removed. ATRX was excluded from all reported differential
expression results.

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis was performed with DESeq2 (v1.46.0; Love et
al., 2014) using a single model (~ condition) fitted to the 12 E6-derived
samples, after removing genes with fewer than 10 reads across samples (18,729 of
28,278 genes retained; 66.2%). Significance was assessed with Wald tests, P
values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method (Benjamini & Hochberg,
1995), and genes with an adjusted P value (FDR) < 0.05 and |log₂ fold-change| ≥
1 were considered differentially expressed.

Principal component analysis (PCA)

Counts were variance-stabilised with DESeq2, and PCA was performed on the 500
most variable genes (centered but not scaled) after removal of ATRX. Sample
similarity was assessed by Spearman correlation of variance-stabilized counts,
excluding ATRX, construct and reporter features.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

Pre-ranked GSEA was performed with fgsea (v1.32.0, fgseaMultilevel; Korotkevich
et al., 2021) on genes ranked by the DESeq2 Wald statistic. Hallmark (Liberzon
et al., 2015) and Gene Ontology Biological Process (GO-BP; Ashburner et al.,
2000) gene sets were obtained from MSigDB (v7.5.1; Liberzon et al., 2011) using
msigdbr (v7.5.1; Dolgalev, 2022). Current human KEGG pathways (Kanehisa & Goto,
2000) were retrieved using KEGGREST (v1.46.0; Tenenbaum, 2017) and mapped to
gene symbols with org.Hs.eg.db (v3.20.0; Carlson, 2017). Two custom gene sets
(all HOX genes; HOXB genes) were added. Gene sets with 9–500 detected genes were
tested and considered significantly enriched at FDR < 0.05. KEGG and GO-BP
results were further grouped into five functional categories (replication and
repair, chromosome, cell growth and death, nervous system, development and
regeneration), defined by the KEGG pathway classification and, for GO-BP, by
keyword matching of term names.

Genes and pathways shared by both ATRX constructs

Genes differentially expressed in the same direction in both construct vs empty
vector comparisons were tested for over-representation in Hallmark, KEGG and
GO-BP gene sets (gene set size range: 10–500) using one-sided hypergeometric
tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, with all genes tested in both
comparisons as the background universe. Given the small number of shared genes,
gene sets with nominal P < 0.05 were reported as exploratory. Pathways were
considered shared when significantly enriched (FDR < 0.05) with the same sign of
the normalised enrichment score in the GSEA of both comparisons.

Integration with co-IP proteomics

Transcriptomic differences between the two ATRX constructs were compared with
proteins differentially enriched between the constructs in the co-IP/MS analysis
(S1744 vs S1011; FDR ≤ 0.05 and |log₂ fold-change| > 1), matched by gene symbol.
RNA and protein changes were considered concordant when their log₂ fold-changes
had the same sign.

Neuroblastoma patient cohort

Bulk RNA-seq data from Rifatbegovic et al. (2018) (see “Data availability”) were
trimmed with fastp (Chen et al., 2018), mapped and quantified to GRCh38.p13
transcriptome with Salmon (Patro et al., 2017), summarised to genes with tximeta
(Love et al., 2020) and filtered with edgeR filterByExpr (Robinson et al., 2010;
Chen et al., 2016), retaining 14,604 of 38,269 genes. PCA was performed on the
2,000 most variable genes among those with log₂ expression > 1 in at least two
samples.

Expression of candidate ATRX interactors

Candidate direct interactors of one or both ATRX constructs were selected from
co-IP/MS-enriched proteins by AlphaFold2 interaction modelling (Jumper et al.,
2021). Expression of the corresponding genes was examined as log₂(normalised
counts + 1) in the E6-derived samples and as limma removeBatchEffect (Ritchie et
al., 2015) corrected log₂ expression in the patient cohort, for each tumour
group and sample set, with control bone marrow shown for reference. Samples from
the same patient within a sample set were averaged.

Differential expression and pathway analysis in patients

Differential expression between the three tumour groups was analysed separately
in each sample set with limma (v3.62.1; Ritchie et al., 2015) on uncorrected
log₂(normalised counts + 1), using limma-trend (Law et al., 2014) with robust
empirical Bayes moderation (Phipson et al., 2016). The design included tumour
group and library layout. Where patients contributed several samples, patient
was treated as a blocking factor, with the within-patient correlation estimated
using duplicateCorrelation (Smyth et al., 2005). Genes with log₂ expression > 1
in at least as many samples as the smallest group were tested. Pairwise
comparisons between groups with at least two samples each were extracted as
contrasts, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and genes
with FDR < 0.05 and |log₂ fold-change| ≥ 1 were considered differentially
expressed.

For each comparison, pre-ranked GSEA was performed with fgsea on genes ranked by
the limma moderated t-statistic, using the Hallmark, GO-BP and KEGG gene sets
and size limits described above.
