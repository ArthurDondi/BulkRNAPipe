BULK RNA-SEQ ANALYSIS

Sequencing and read processing
<Cell lines, constructs, RNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing to
be added.> RNA from five conditions (TP53 knockout; TP53/ATRX double knockout
clone E6; E6 transduced with an empty vector, with ATRX full length, or with an
ATRX in-frame fusion construct; three replicates each) was sequenced as 150-bp
paired-end, reverse-stranded reads. Read quality was assessed with FastQC
(v0.12.1; Andrews, 2010) and MultiQC (v1.25.2; Ewels et al., 2016) before and
after trimming. Adapters and low-quality bases (Phred < 20) were removed with
Trim Galore (v0.6.10; Krueger, 2023) and Cutadapt (v4.9; Martin, 2011), and
reads shorter than 20 nt were discarded. Reads were aligned with STAR (v2.7.11b;
Dobin et al., 2013) in two-pass mode to the human genome (hg38, <annotation
source and release>) supplemented with the sequences of the two ATRX constructs
and of the EGFP and mCherry reporters. Read pairs overlapping exons were counted
per gene with featureCounts (Subread v2.0.6; Liao et al., 2014), retaining
uniquely mapped reads with mapping quality ≥ 10. The workflow was implemented in
Snakemake (v8; Mölder et al., 2021), and all downstream analyses were performed
in R (v4.4.2; R Core Team, 2024), with figures generated using ggplot2 (v3.5.1;
Wickham, 2016).

Quantification of ATRX expression
Reads from the ATRX constructs map equally well to the endogenous ATRX locus and
are therefore discarded as multi-mapping reads by unique counting. Total ATRX
expression was quantified by pooling the exons of endogenous ATRX and of both
constructs into a single feature and re-counting the alignments with
multi-mapping reads fractionally assigned (featureCounts -M --fraction).
Expression of the two constructs was compared using reads overlapping (≥ 25 nt)
the Twin-Strep/10×His tag sequence, which is shared by both constructs and
absent from the human genome. For all downstream analyses, the pooled ATRX count
(rounded to integers) replaced the endogenous and construct features, and the
reporter features were removed. ATRX was excluded from all reported differential
expression results.

Differential expression analysis
Differential expression analysis was performed with DESeq2 (v1.46.0; Love et
al., 2014) using a single model fitted to all 15 samples (~ condition), after
removing genes with fewer than 10 reads across samples (<N> of <N> genes
retained). Contrasts compared E6 with the TP53 knockout, the empty vector
control with E6, each ATRX construct with the empty vector control and with the
TP53 knockout, and the two ATRX constructs with each other. Significance was
assessed with Wald tests, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg
method (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995), and genes with an adjusted P value (FDR) <
0.05 and |log2 fold-change| ≥ 1 were considered differentially expressed. As the
TP53 knockout line and the E6 lineage derive from different clones, ATRX status
is confounded with clonal background in comparisons against the TP53 knockout;
comparisons within the E6 lineage were therefore used as primary readouts.

Principal component analysis (PCA)
Counts were variance-stabilised with DESeq2 (blind to the experimental design),
and PCA was performed on the 500 most variable genes (centred but not scaled)
after removal of ATRX, for all samples and for the 12 E6-derived samples
separately. Sample similarity was assessed by Spearman correlation of
variance-stabilised counts, excluding ATRX, construct and reporter features.

Signature reversal
Rescue by each ATRX construct was quantified as the reversal of the ATRX
knockout signature, using only comparisons within a single genetic background.
The knockout signature comprised genes with FDR < 0.05 and |log2 fold-change| ≥
1 in E6 vs TP53 knockout (<N> genes), or with FDR < 0.05 alone in a sensitivity
analysis. For these genes, log2 fold-changes of each construct vs the empty
vector control were compared with those of the knockout using the total least
squares regression slope (Golub & Van Loan, 1980; −1 indicating complete
reversal), the Spearman correlation, and the fraction of genes changing in the
opposite direction. The latter was tested against a null distribution obtained
by permuting the construct log2 fold-changes across signature genes (1,000
permutations). As a negative control, the vector transduction signature (empty
vector vs E6) was scored against the in-frame fusion vs full length comparison.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)
Pre-ranked GSEA (Subramanian et al., 2005) was performed with fgsea (v1.32.0,
fgseaMultilevel; Korotkevich et al., 2021) on genes ranked by the DESeq2 Wald
statistic. Hallmark (Liberzon et al., 2015) and Gene Ontology Biological Process
(GO-BP; Ashburner et al., 2000) gene sets were obtained from MSigDB (v7.5.1;
Liberzon et al., 2011) using msigdbr (v7.5.1; Dolgalev, 2022). Current human
KEGG pathways (Kanehisa & Goto, 2000) were retrieved using KEGGREST (<version>;
Tenenbaum, 2017; retrieved <date>) and mapped to gene symbols with org.Hs.eg.db
(v3.20.0; Carlson, 2017). Two custom gene sets (all HOX genes; HOXB genes) were
added. Gene sets with 9–500 detected genes were tested and considered
significantly enriched at FDR < 0.05. For the comparison between the two ATRX
constructs, KEGG and GO-BP results were further grouped into five functional
categories (replication and repair, chromosome, cell growth and death, nervous
system, development and regeneration), defined by the KEGG pathway
classification and, for GO-BP, by keyword matching of term names.

Genes and pathways shared by both ATRX constructs
Genes differentially expressed in the same direction in both construct vs empty
vector comparisons were tested for over-representation in Hallmark, KEGG and
GO-BP gene sets (gene set size range: 10–500) using one-sided hypergeometric
tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, with all genes tested in both
comparisons as the background universe. Given the small number of shared genes,
gene sets with nominal P < 0.05 were reported as exploratory. Pathways were
considered shared when significantly enriched (FDR < 0.05) with the same sign of
the normalised enrichment score in the GSEA of both comparisons.

Integration with co-IP proteomics
Transcriptomic differences between the two ATRX constructs were compared with
proteins differentially enriched between the constructs in the co-IP/MS analysis
(S1744 vs S1011; FDR ≤ 0.05 and |log2 fold-change| > 1), matched by gene symbol.
RNA and protein changes were considered concordant when their log2 fold-changes
had the same sign.

Expression of candidate ATRX interactors
Candidate direct interactors of each ATRX construct were selected from
co-IP/MS-enriched proteins by AlphaFold2 interaction modelling (Jumper et al.,
2021; <reference to methods>). Expression of the corresponding genes was
examined as log2(normalised counts + 1) in the RNA-seq samples described above
and in an in-house neuroblastoma bulk RNA-seq cohort (<reference; ethics
approval>). Cohort data were previously trimmed with fastp (Chen et al., 2018),
quantified with Salmon (Patro et al., 2017) and summarised to genes with tximeta
(Love et al., 2020), filtered with edgeR filterByExpr (Robinson et al., 2010;
Chen et al., 2016), and corrected for library layout with limma
removeBatchEffect (Ritchie et al., 2015), with sample material retained in the
design. Primary tumour and disseminated tumour cell samples were grouped by ATRX
deletion and, for ATRX wild-type samples, by MYCN amplification status, and by
time point (diagnosis, treatment, relapse), with tumour-free bone marrow as
control. Samples with ambiguous ATRX or MYCN status (ATRX gain, Xq loss,
heterogeneous MYCN amplification) or missing annotation were excluded, samples
from the same patient were averaged, and groups with two or fewer patients were
not shown (<N> samples from <N> patients included).
