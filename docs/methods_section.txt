METHODS: BULK RNA-SEQ ANALYSIS
==============================
Epicode: ATRX rescue in an ATRX-knockout neuroblastoma line

Two versions of the bulk RNA-seq methods follow: a concise version for the main
text and a detailed version for supplementary methods. Both have the same nine
subsections, in the order of the nine sections of the analysis report. [TO FILL:
...] marks information that is not recorded in the analysis code and must be
completed before submission.


CONCISE VERSION
===============

1. Experimental design and samples
----------------------------------
(Report section 1: "Experimental design")

Bulk RNA-seq was performed on five SK-N-SH-derived conditions with three
replicates each (n = 15): a TP53-knockout line (TP53), a TP53/ATRX
double-knockout clone (E6), and E6 cells transduced with an empty EGFP/mCherry
reporter vector (EmptyVector), a vector encoding full-length ATRX (ATRX_FL) or a
vector encoding an ATRX in-frame fusion (ATRX_IFF). [TO FILL: cell line
generation, RNA extraction, library preparation kit and sequencing platform.]
Libraries were sequenced as 150-bp paired-end, reverse-stranded reads. Because
the TP53 line and the E6 lineage differ in clonal background as well as in ATRX
status, comparisons within the E6 lineage were treated as primary. The workflow
was implemented in Snakemake v8 [1] with Bioconda environments [2].


2. Read processing, quantification and quality control
------------------------------------------------------
(Report section 2: "Quality control")

Read quality was assessed with FastQC v0.12.1 [3] and MultiQC v1.25.2 [4] before
and after trimming. Adapters and low-quality bases (Phred < 20) were removed
with Trim Galore v0.6.10 [5] and Cutadapt v4.9 [6], discarding reads shorter
than 20 nt. Reads were aligned with STAR v2.7.11b [7] in two-pass mode to hg38
supplemented with the ATRX_FL, ATRX_IFF, EGFP and mCherry sequences [TO FILL:
genome and annotation source and release]. Uniquely aligned fragments (MAPQ ≥
10) were counted per gene with featureCounts (Subread v2.0.6) [8]. Because
construct-derived reads multi-map to the endogenous ATRX locus, total ATRX
expression was quantified by pooling the three ATRX features into one
meta-feature and counting multi-mapping fragments fractionally; expression of
the two constructs was compared using fragments overlapping their shared
Twin-Strep/10×His tag sequence. For downstream analyses the pooled ATRX count
replaced the three separate ATRX features, and EGFP and mCherry were removed.
Library size, assignment rate, number of detected genes and Spearman
correlations between samples on variance-stabilised counts were inspected by
condition. Statistical analyses used R v4.4.2 [9].


3. Principal component analysis
-------------------------------
(Report section 3: "Global structure (PCA)")

Counts of genes with at least 10 reads in total were variance-stabilised with
DESeq2 v1.46.0 (blind to the design) [10,11] after removing ATRX, and principal
component analysis was computed on the 500 most variable genes.


4. Differential expression analysis
-----------------------------------
(Report section 4: "How TP53 differs from the E6 lineage")

Differential expression was tested with DESeq2 [10] on all 15 samples with the
design ~ condition, after removing genes with fewer than 10 reads in total.
Pairwise contrasts were tested with Wald tests and P values were adjusted with
the Benjamini–Hochberg method [12]; genes with adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2 fold
change| ≥ 1 were considered differentially expressed. The contrasts were E6 vs
TP53, EmptyVector vs E6, ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector, ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector,
ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, ATRX_FL vs TP53 and ATRX_IFF vs TP53. ATRX itself was
excluded from differential-expression figures and tables.


5. Rescue assessment by signature reversal
------------------------------------------
(Report section 5: "Rescue assessment")

Rescue was scored as reversal of the knockout signature, using only contrasts
within one genetic background. The signature comprised genes with adjusted P <
0.05 in E6 vs TP53, with or without an additional |log2 fold change| ≥ 1
requirement. For these genes, the log2 fold change of ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector or
of ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector was compared with the knockout log2 fold change using
the total-least-squares slope [13] (−1 indicating complete reversal), the
Spearman correlation and the percentage of genes changing in the opposite
direction. This percentage was compared with a null distribution obtained by
permuting the response fold changes across signature genes 1,000 times. As a
negative control, the EmptyVector vs E6 signature was scored against the
ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL response.


6. Analyses restricted to the E6 lineage
----------------------------------------
(Report section 6: "The E6 lineage alone")

Principal component analysis was repeated on the 12 E6-derived samples.
Construct effects were taken from the ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector, ATRX_IFF vs
EmptyVector and ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL contrasts of the model described above. For
ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, RNA changes were compared, by gene symbol, with the
proteins that differed between the two constructs in the co-immunoprecipitation
mass spectrometry data (limma [14]; FDR ≤ 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 1) [TO
FILL: reference to the proteomics methods]. Expression of HOXB2, HOXB4, HOXB6,
HOXB7 and IGF2BP1 is shown as mean ± s.d. of DESeq2-normalised counts.


7. Gene set enrichment analysis
-------------------------------
(Report section 7: "Pathway enrichment (GSEA)")

Pre-ranked gene set enrichment analysis [15] was run with fgsea v1.32.0 [16] on
genes ranked by the DESeq2 Wald statistic, using MSigDB [17] Hallmark [18] and
Gene Ontology biological process [19] gene sets (msigdbr v7.5.1 [20]) and
current KEGG pathways [21] retrieved with KEGGREST [22]. Gene sets with 9–500
detected genes were tested and those with Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05
were considered significant. For ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, KEGG pathways and GO terms
were additionally examined in five predefined categories (replication and
repair; chromosome; cell growth and death; nervous system; development and
regeneration), defined by the KEGG pathway classification and, for GO, by
keyword matching on term names.


8. Genes and pathways shared by the two ATRX constructs
-------------------------------------------------------
(Report section 8: "Shared by ATRX_FL and ATRX_IFF")

Genes differentially expressed in the same direction in both ATRX_FL vs
EmptyVector and ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector were tested for over-representation in
Hallmark, KEGG and GO biological process gene sets (10–500 genes) with a
one-sided hypergeometric test against all genes tested in both contrasts, with
Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment. This analysis was exploratory, and gene sets with
nominal P < 0.05 are reported. Pathways were considered shared when significant
(adjusted P < 0.05) with the same sign of the normalised enrichment score in the
enrichment analyses of both contrasts.


9. Expression of candidate ATRX interactors
-------------------------------------------
(Report section 9: "ATRX interactors")

Expression of genes encoding candidate ATRX_FL and ATRX_IFF interactors,
identified by co-immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry and AlphaFold2 [23]
interaction modelling [TO FILL: reference to these methods], was examined as
log2(normalised counts + 1) in the SK-N-SH samples and in an in-house
neuroblastoma cohort [TO FILL: cohort reference and ethics approval]. The cohort
data had been processed with fastp [24], Salmon [25] and tximeta [26], filtered
with edgeR filterByExpr [27,28] and corrected for library layout with limma
removeBatchEffect [14]. Tumour and disseminated tumour cell samples were grouped
as ATRX-deleted, MYCN-amplified or MYCN-non-amplified (the latter two ATRX wild
type) and by time point (diagnosis, treatment or relapse), with tumour-cell-free
bone marrow as control. Libraries from the same patient were averaged, and
categories with two or fewer patients are not shown.


DETAILED VERSION
================

1. Experimental design and samples
----------------------------------
(Report section 1: "Experimental design")

Bulk RNA-seq was performed on five conditions derived from the neuroblastoma
cell line SK-N-SH: a TP53-knockout line (TP53); a TP53/ATRX double-knockout
clone (E6); and E6 cells transduced with an empty vector carrying EGFP and
mCherry reporters (EmptyVector, construct 1760), a vector encoding full-length
ATRX (ATRX_FL, construct 1011; 7,482-bp cDNA) or a vector encoding an ATRX
in-frame fusion (ATRX_IFF, construct 1744; 3,993-bp cDNA). Both ATRX constructs
are cDNAs without introns or untranslated regions and carry an N-terminal
Twin-Strep/10×His tag. Each condition was sequenced in triplicate, giving 15
libraries [TO FILL: whether replicates are independent cultures; cell line
generation, transduction, RNA extraction, library preparation kit, sequencing
platform and depth]. Libraries were sequenced as 150-bp paired-end,
reverse-stranded reads.

Each condition is a single clone or a single transduced pool, so the TP53 line
and the E6 lineage differ in clonal background as well as in ATRX genotype, and
the two cannot be separated or modelled as a covariate in this design.
Comparisons within the E6 lineage (EmptyVector, ATRX_FL, ATRX_IFF) were
therefore treated as primary, comparisons against TP53 are reported for
completeness, and no batch correction was applied before any statistical test.
The workflow was implemented in Snakemake v8 [1] with one Conda environment per
step built from Bioconda and conda-forge [2], and is available at
https://github.com/ArthurDondi/BulkRNAPipe [TO FILL: release tag or commit].


2. Read processing, quantification and quality control
------------------------------------------------------
(Report section 2: "Quality control")

Read quality was assessed with FastQC v0.12.1 [3] and summarised with MultiQC
v1.25.2 [4], before and after trimming. Adapters and low-quality bases were
trimmed with Trim Galore v0.6.10 [5] and Cutadapt v4.9 [6] in paired-end mode
with automatic adapter detection, a Phred quality cut-off of 20 and a minimum
read length of 20 nt. The reference consisted of the human genome hg38 with the
ATRX_FL, ATRX_IFF, EGFP and mCherry sequences added as separate contigs, with a
matching gene annotation in which genes are identified by symbol [TO FILL:
genome assembly and annotation source and release]. Reads were aligned with STAR
v2.7.11b [7] in two-pass mode (--twopassMode Basic), using an index built with
the annotation and --sjdbOverhang 149; alignments were coordinate-sorted, and
indexed with SAMtools v1.21 [29].

Fragments were assigned to genes with featureCounts (Subread v2.0.6) [8] in
paired-end, reverse-stranded mode (-p --countReadPairs -s 2) with a minimum
mapping quality of 10, counting exon-overlapping fragments per gene and
discarding multi-mapping fragments. Under this rule the endogenous ATRX gene and
the two constructs are each represented only by fragments that can be assigned
to one of them unambiguously (untranslated-region or exon–intron-spanning
fragments for the endogenous gene; read pairs joining the tag to
construct-specific sequence for ATRX_FL; read pairs spanning the fusion
breakpoint for ATRX_IFF). Two additional features were therefore counted from
the same alignments with multi-mapping fragments included and weighted
fractionally (-Q 0 -M --fraction): total ATRX, for which the exons of endogenous
ATRX, ATRX_FL and ATRX_IFF were pooled into a single meta-feature; and the tag,
defined as nucleotides 1–117 of both construct contigs, with a minimum overlap
of 25 nt. The tag sequence is absent from the human genome and identical in both
constructs, and was used to compare their expression.

For quality control, size factors were estimated with the DESeq2 v1.46.0
median-of-ratios method [10,11] in R v4.4.2 [9] after excluding the endogenous
ATRX gene, the two constructs and the two reporters, and were then applied to
all features. The number of assigned fragments, the percentage of fragments
assigned and the number of genes with at least one count were compared across
conditions. Spearman correlations between samples were computed on
variance-stabilised counts (DESeq2 vst, blind to the design) [11] of genes with
at least 10 reads in total, excluding the same five features. For all downstream
analyses, a derived count matrix was used in which the total ATRX count, rounded
to the nearest integer, replaced the three separate ATRX features, and EGFP and
mCherry were removed. Figures were drawn with ggplot2 [30] and the report was
generated with R Markdown [31].


3. Principal component analysis
-------------------------------
(Report section 3: "Global structure (PCA)")

Principal component analysis was performed on all 15 samples from the derived
count matrix. ATRX was removed before normalisation, because it is the variable
under study, so that size factors and the transformation were estimated on the
retained genes. Genes with fewer than 10 reads in total were removed, counts
were variance-stabilised (DESeq2 vst, blind to the design) [10,11], and
principal components were computed on the centred, unscaled values of the 500
genes with the highest variance.


4. Differential expression analysis
-----------------------------------
(Report section 4: "How TP53 differs from the E6 lineage")

Differential expression was analysed with DESeq2 v1.46.0 [10] on the derived
count matrix. All 15 samples were fitted in a single model with the design ~
condition, after removing genes with fewer than 10 reads in total across
samples, so that size factors and dispersions were estimated from all
conditions. Seven pairwise contrasts (numerator vs denominator) were extracted
with Wald tests: E6 vs TP53, EmptyVector vs E6, ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector, ATRX_IFF
vs EmptyVector, ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, ATRX_FL vs TP53 and ATRX_IFF vs TP53. P
values were adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg method [12], with independent
filtering at α = 0.05, and log2 fold changes are reported without shrinkage.
Genes with adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1 were considered
differentially expressed.

The size of each effect was summarised as the number of genes higher and lower
in the numerator, and for E6 vs TP53 as a proportion of the genes with an
adjusted P value. In tables and volcano plots, differentially expressed genes
were ranked by −log10(adjusted P) × |log2 fold change|, separately for each
direction. ATRX, which in the derived matrix is the pooled count of the
endogenous gene and both constructs, was excluded from all
differential-expression figures and tables.


5. Rescue assessment by signature reversal
------------------------------------------
(Report section 5: "Rescue assessment")

Because the ATRX-reconstituted cells and the TP53 line differ in clonal
background and transduction status, rescue was not assessed by comparing them
directly but by signature reversal, using two contrasts that each lie within one
background. The knockout signature comprised genes with adjusted P < 0.05 in E6
vs TP53, either without a fold-change requirement or with |log2 fold change| ≥
1. For signature genes with a finite log2 fold change in both contrasts, the
response log2 fold change (ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector or ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector)
was compared with the signature log2 fold change. The slope was estimated by
total-least-squares (orthogonal) regression [13], as the first principal axis of
the centred pairs, because both variables are estimated with error; a slope of
−1 indicates complete reversal and 0 no response. The Spearman correlation, the
squared Pearson correlation and the percentage of signature genes with a
response of opposite sign were also computed.

The percentage of reversed genes was compared with a null distribution obtained
by permuting the response log2 fold changes across signature genes 1,000 times
(fixed random seed). The excess over the null is the observed percentage minus
the mean permuted percentage, and the one-sided permutation P value is (b +
1)/(1,000 + 1), where b is the number of permutations with a percentage at least
as large as observed. Signature and response contrasts were chosen to share no
condition, because a shared group induces a spurious negative correlation
between the two fold changes. As a negative control, the vector-transduction
signature (EmptyVector vs E6; adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1) was
scored against the ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL response, for which no reversal is
expected.


6. Analyses restricted to the E6 lineage
----------------------------------------
(Report section 6: "The E6 lineage alone")

To hold clone and transduction constant, the TP53 samples were excluded.
Principal component analysis was repeated as described above on the 12
E6-derived samples (E6, EmptyVector, ATRX_FL and ATRX_IFF), with size factors,
variance stabilisation and the selection of the 500 most variable genes
recomputed on this subset. Sample correlations are the Spearman correlations
described above, restricted to these samples. Construct effects were taken from
the ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector, ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector and ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL
contrasts of the 15-sample model, with the same significance thresholds.

For ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, transcript changes were compared with the
co-immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry data (label-free quantification,
analysed with limma [14]) [TO FILL: reference to the proteomics methods].
Proteins with FDR ≤ 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 1 in the ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL
comparison were matched to the RNA-seq results by gene symbol, keeping the entry
with the lowest FDR when a gene was listed more than once. RNA and protein were
considered concordant when their log2 fold changes had the same sign. Expression
of HOXB2, HOXB4, HOXB6, HOXB7 and IGF2BP1 is shown as the mean ± s.d. of
DESeq2-normalised counts of the three replicates per condition.


7. Gene set enrichment analysis
-------------------------------
(Report section 7: "Pathway enrichment (GSEA)")

Pre-ranked gene set enrichment analysis [15] was performed for each contrast
with the fgseaMultilevel function of fgsea v1.32.0 [16] (nPermSimple = 1,000;
fixed random seed). All genes with a DESeq2 Wald statistic were ranked by that
statistic, so that a positive normalised enrichment score (NES) indicates higher
expression in the numerator of the contrast. Hallmark [18] and Gene Ontology
biological process [19] gene sets were taken from MSigDB v7.5.1 [17] through
msigdbr v7.5.1 [20]. Because the KEGG collection distributed with MSigDB does
not include pathways added to KEGG more recently, current human KEGG pathways
[21] were retrieved from the KEGG REST interface with KEGGREST [22] and gene
identifiers were converted to symbols with org.Hs.eg.db v3.20.0 [32] [TO FILL:
date of KEGG retrieval and KEGGREST version]. Two custom gene sets, containing
all detected HOXA–HOXD genes and the detected HOXB genes, were added to each
collection. Gene sets with 9–500 genes present in the ranked list were tested,
and P values were adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg method [12] within each
contrast and collection; gene sets with adjusted P < 0.05 were considered
significant. Hallmark results are shown for ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, ATRX_FL vs
EmptyVector and ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector, as up to 12 significant gene sets per
direction ranked by |NES|, together with their leading-edge genes.

For ATRX_IFF vs ATRX_FL, KEGG and GO results were additionally examined in five
predefined categories: replication and repair; chromosome; cell growth and
death; nervous system; and development and regeneration. KEGG pathways were
assigned to these categories according to the KEGG pathway classification (32
human pathways in total). GO has no equivalent grouping, so GO terms were
assigned by case-insensitive regular expressions matched against term names
(listed in the code repository), and up to 25 significant terms per category are
shown, ranked by adjusted P. The adjusted P values are those of the
collection-wide analysis and were not re-adjusted within categories.


8. Genes and pathways shared by the two ATRX constructs
-------------------------------------------------------
(Report section 8: "Shared by ATRX_FL and ATRX_IFF")

To identify changes common to both ATRX constructs, genes that were
differentially expressed (adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1) in the
same direction in ATRX_FL vs EmptyVector and in ATRX_IFF vs EmptyVector were
selected, excluding ATRX. Genes higher in both constructs and genes lower in
both constructs were tested separately for over-representation in Hallmark,
current KEGG and GO biological process gene sets with a one-sided hypergeometric
test. The universe consisted of all genes with an adjusted P value in both
contrasts; gene sets were restricted to the universe and those with 10–500 genes
were tested. P values were adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg method [12]
within each direction and collection over the gene sets containing at least one
shared gene, as in clusterProfiler [33]. Fold enrichment is the observed overlap
divided by the overlap expected by chance. Because the shared gene lists are
short, this analysis was considered exploratory: gene sets with nominal P < 0.05
are reported (up to 20 per direction and collection), and those that also have
adjusted P < 0.05 are indicated.

At the pathway level, the enrichment results of the two contrasts (described
above) were compared for each collection. A pathway was considered shared when
it had adjusted P < 0.05 in both contrasts with the same sign of the NES.


9. Expression of candidate ATRX interactors
-------------------------------------------
(Report section 9: "ATRX interactors")

Candidate interactors of ATRX_FL and ATRX_IFF were identified by
co-immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry, and the top hits were tested for
direct interaction in silico with AlphaFold2 [23] and classified as interactors
(“Yes”) or possible interactors (“Maybe?”) according to the interaction score
[TO FILL: reference to the proteomics and structure-prediction methods]. The
current list comprises 38 genes: 12 for ATRX_FL (8 “Yes”, 4 “Maybe?”) and 26 for
ATRX_IFF (10 “Yes”, 16 “Maybe?”) [TO FILL: update once the list is final]. In
the SK-N-SH data, expression of these genes is shown as log2(DESeq2-normalised
counts + 1), with genes matched by current or previous HGNC symbol.

Expression in patients was examined in an in-house neuroblastoma bulk RNA-seq
cohort [TO FILL: cohort reference, sample numbers and ethics approval]. These
data had previously been trimmed with fastp [24], quantified with Salmon [25]
and summarised to genes with tximeta [26]; genes were filtered with edgeR
filterByExpr [27,28] (14,604 of 38,269 genes retained), and the library-layout
effect (single-end unstranded, single-end stranded or paired-end) was removed
with limma removeBatchEffect [14], with the sample material kept in the design.
Genes were matched by Ensembl gene identifier, then by current and previous
symbol. Negative values produced by the correction were set to zero, and values
are shown as log2(corrected value + 1).

Primary tumour and disseminated tumour cell samples were assigned to three
groups: ATRX-deleted (irrespective of MYCN status), MYCN-amplified and
MYCN-non-amplified (the latter two with wild-type ATRX). Bone marrow samples
without tumour cells served as controls. Samples with ATRX gain, Xq loss or
heterogeneous MYCN amplification, samples without ATRX/MYCN status, material
annotation or time point, samples removed during preprocessing or flagged for
exclusion in the cohort metadata, and mononuclear-cell and cell-line samples
were excluded. Samples were further divided by time point (diagnosis, treatment
or relapse). Libraries from the same patient within a category were averaged, so
that each patient contributes one value, and categories with two or fewer
patients are not shown. This analysis is descriptive, and no statistical test
was applied.
